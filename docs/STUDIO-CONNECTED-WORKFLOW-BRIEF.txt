# Aegis Studio — Complete Workflow Implementation Brief

**Project:** Aegis.Studio  
**Priority:** High  
**Revision:** October 8, 2026 — approved decisions integrated  
**Implementation scope:** Booking → FrontDesk → Workspaces → Jobs → Photographer Assignment → Scheduling → Photoshoot → Upload → Production  
**Objective:** Establish a complete, connected operational workflow across Studio while preserving its existing design and functionality.

**Decision precedence:** This revision replaces the earlier brief. Section 21 provides the binding implementation contracts and resolves legacy code or documentation conflicts. Preserve working data and integrations while replacing the operational flow as specified. Deposit eligibility uses any recorded positive deposit in this phase; business-hours timing and advanced deposit policy are deferred.

**Instructions to the development agent:** Implement the requirements below. Inspect the existing code before making changes, reuse functioning components, and complete the work in logical stages. Do not stop after producing an implementation plan.

## 1. Fundamental design principles

Aegis Studio must operate as one integrated business management system.

**Today is the visual design reference for the entire application.**

Preserve its existing appearance pixel-for-pixel wherever practical.

Requirements:

1. Maintain the existing Studio visual design, typography, spacing, rounded corners, and compact layouts.
2. Apply the established Today grid and scrolling behavior consistently to other operational pages.
3. Preserve existing functionality.
4. Avoid duplicate business logic, workflow states, and data.
5. Use the existing authentication, messaging, storage, and notification infrastructure.
6. Ensure every page has reliable navigation to related pages.
7. Make all workflow actions available from their appropriate operational context.
8. Keep Jobs as the authoritative source for job workflow state.
9. Implement role-based permissions for managerial actions.
10. Preserve existing working integrations with Common libraries.

Do not redesign Today, Workspaces, Production, or Jobs unnecessarily.

**The objective is functional integration, not visual reinvention.**

# 3. Booking and pending jobs

A customer submits a booking through Brian's website.

The booking creates a real Studio Job immediately.

Initial workflow state:

`Pending`

Create the authoritative Pending Job immediately. Retain any existing booking/request record only as linked intake or financial provenance, never as a later source of a second Job. Migrate existing pending bookings idempotently to this model.

### Booking information

Capture:

- Customer
- Contact information
- Requested service
- Preferred date and time
- Booking notes
- Destination email address
- Booking source
- Workspace
- Deposit requirement
- Submission timestamp

Use a stable external booking identifier and idempotent submission handling to prevent duplicate jobs.

### Workspace routing

The destination email address determines the initial workspace.

Example:

```text
weddings@bryanstudiosltd.com
             ↓
       Wedding Workspace
```

Mappings must be configurable.

If no workspace mapping exists:

`Workspace = the predefined permanent Unassigned Workspace`

The job enters FrontDesk's Unassigned Jobs queue. Seed the permanent Unassigned Workspace once under a stable system identifier; retries must not duplicate it. Prevent deletion, disabling, or reassignment of its system identity. It is an intake container managed by FrontDesk, not a newly created workspace for each booking.

FrontDesk can assign or correct the workspace.

**Do not automatically create a new workspace for each booking.**

# 4. FrontDesk workflow

FrontDesk owns the initial processing of pending jobs.

Pending Jobs is one operational channel within FrontDesk.

### Pending job actions

FrontDesk employees must be able to:

1. View pending jobs.
2. Claim a job for processing.
3. Review booking details.
4. Assign or correct the workspace.
5. Contact the customer.
6. Check scheduling availability.
7. Review deposit requirements.
8. Confirm eligible bookings.
9. Cancel bookings with a reason.
10. Escalate to the operations manager.

### Booking confirmation rules

For now, there are two permitted confirmation paths.

**Path A — Deposit received**

Any positive deposit has been recorded against the booking or Job by an authorized FrontDesk employee. For this phase, any recorded deposit satisfies confirmation eligibility regardless of the configured required amount. Do not implement minimum-amount enforcement, provider verification, credit, or pay-later policy. Preserve amounts, currency, receipts, and audit records using existing financial infrastructure.

FrontDesk confirms the booking.

**Path B — Operations manager override**

The operations manager authorizes the booking to proceed without the required deposit.

Record:

- Authorizing manager
- Timestamp
- Reason
- Original deposit requirement

An override must not incorrectly mark the deposit as paid.

Maintain separate payment and job workflow states.

**Deferred:** Customer credit arrangements and pay-later account privileges.

Do not implement customer credit policy during this phase.

### Confirmation outcome

Once confirmed, record the **Booking Confirmed event** and immediately transition:

```text
Pending → Needs Assignment
```

Confirmed is an event, not a persistent workflow stage. A real business workspace is required before confirmation; Jobs in Unassigned remain Pending.

The confirmed job remains within its assigned workspace.

The workspace manager receives responsibility for ensuring assignment.

# 5. Job responsibility model

Implement three distinct responsibilities.

| Responsibility | Function |
|---|---|
| Workspace Manager | Oversight and managerial accountability |
| Job Owner | Coordination and execution of the job |
| Photographer | Photography assignment and execution |

One employee may hold multiple responsibilities.

For example, a small portrait session may have one person acting as job owner and photographer.

A wedding may require a workspace manager, job owner, lead photographer, and several additional photographers.

### Assignment workflow

After confirmation:

1. The job enters Needs Assignment (displayed as Needs Job Owner where helpful).
2. The workspace manager is notified.
3. The job appears in the workspace's Needs Assignment view.
4. The manager assigns a job owner.
5. After a Job Owner is assigned, the job enters Needs Photographer Assignment.
6. Once the planned shoot crew is complete, the job enters Awaiting Scheduling until all required shoot sessions have valid dates, times, and locations.
7. The job owner or workspace manager arranges photographer assignments and scheduling using the same Job records.

Job ownership and photographer assignment must remain separate.

# 6. Photographer assignment — immediate implementation priority

**Implement the photographer assignment interface as a priority.**

Photographer assignments must be managed primarily from the Job page.

### Required action

`Assign Photographers`

The action opens a dedicated assignment panel or page.

### Multi-photographer support

Implement multi-photographer assignment immediately.

Each Job may have multiple shoot sessions. Each session has:

- Exactly one lead photographer once its crew is finalized.
- Any number of additional photographers.
- Its own schedule, location, crew, execution, and media-readiness records.

Assign people before scheduling if needed. Crew members may share the session time by default; support individual attendance windows inside that session. See Section 21.3 for completion and migration rules.

Assignment fields:

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Photographer | Employee selection |
| Assignment Role | Lead / Additional |
| Scheduled Date | Optional during assignment; required before scheduling |
| Start Time | Optional during assignment; required before scheduling |
| End Time | Optional during assignment; must be after start before scheduling |
| Location | Optional during assignment; required before scheduling |
| Assigned By | Manager or job owner |
| Notes | Instructions |
| Assignment Status | Current scheduling state |

The system must detect scheduling conflicts.

Allow authorized users to resolve conflicts through an explicit decision rather than silently overwriting existing assignments.

### Permissions

Either the workspace manager or job owner may assign photographers.

All assignment changes must be recorded.

### Photographer confirmation

Do not implement an accept/decline workflow inside Studio.

Photographers can communicate scheduling conflicts directly to management.

The manager or authorized job owner adjusts the assignment.

Preserve existing WorkDay photographer confirmation functionality where present, but it must not gate Studio assignment or scheduling. Further confirmation improvements are outside this phase.

Do not make the present implementation dependent on that future functionality.

# 11. Jobs page — workflow status lights

**This is a major new requirement.**

The Jobs page must visually communicate the current operational state of every job.

Each job must display:

1. Current workflow stage.
2. Activity status light.
3. Time spent in the current stage.
4. Responsible person or team.
5. Next required action.

### Example Jobs grid

| Job | Customer | Current Stage | Time in Stage | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| J-1042 | Morgan Wedding | Photographer Assignment | 2 hours | 🟢 |
| J-1043 | Taylor Portrait | Awaiting Production | 9 hours | 🟡 |
| J-1044 | Campbell Event | Production Review | 3 days | 🔴 |
| J-1045 | Jordan Graduation | Scheduled | 1 day | 🟢 |

These values are illustrative, not real Studio records.

### Status light meanings

🟢 **Green — Normal**

The job is progressing within its expected time allowance.

🟡 **Amber — Attention**

The job is approaching or has crossed a warning threshold.

🔴 **Red — Overdue**

The job has exceeded its permitted time in the current workflow state.

⚪ **Grey — Inactive**

The job is cancelled, closed, or otherwise inactive.

Use icons and text alongside colors for accessibility.

### Time in state

The system must record when a job enters each workflow state.

Example:

```text
Current State: Awaiting Production Review
Entered State: October 6, 2026, 10:30 AM
Elapsed Time: 1 day, 22 hours
Status: RED
```

Calculate elapsed time from authoritative timestamps.

Do not reset the timer merely because someone opens or edits the job.

Only a genuine workflow transition should change the state's entry timestamp.

### Configurable thresholds

Different stages require different turnaround times.

Implement configurable warning and overdue thresholds.

Illustrative initial values:

| Workflow Stage | Amber After | Red After |
|---|---:|---:|
| Pending FrontDesk Review | 2 hours | 8 hours |
| Pending in Unassigned Workspace | 2 hours | 8 hours |
| Needs Job Owner | 4 hours | 1 day |
| Needs Photographer Assignment | 4 hours | 1 day |
| Awaiting Scheduling | 8 hours | 2 days |
| Awaiting Production Review | 4 hours | 1 day |
| Active Production | 1 day | 3 days |
| Awaiting Approval | 1 day | 3 days |

**These are proposed defaults, not agreed business service levels.**

Administrators must be able to change them.

Use a regular elapsed clock in this phase: UTC timestamps and continuous elapsed time, including nights, weekends, and holidays. Business-hours calendars, holidays, and pause-adjusted service clocks are deferred. Do not implement them or test them as current functionality.

Scheduled jobs must not become overdue simply because their future photoshoot date has not arrived. Each state requires an appropriate clock-start and timing policy.

### Workflow visibility

Clicking a job's workflow indicator should reveal its progression.

Example:

```text
JOB J-1044

✓ Booking Received
✓ FrontDesk Confirmed
✓ Workspace Assigned
✓ Job Owner Assigned
✓ Photographers Assigned
✓ Shoot Scheduled
✓ Photography Completed
✓ Images Uploaded

● Production Review
  Status: OVERDUE
  Time in Stage: 3 days
  Responsible: Production

○ Production Execution
○ Approval
○ Delivery
○ Completion
```

This should be a compact timeline or expandable panel, consistent with Studio styling.

# 16. Workflow architecture

Implement the workflow as a coherent state model.

Recommended principles:

- One authoritative Job aggregate or equivalent existing domain model.
- Explicit workflow transitions.
- Transition validation.
- Permission checks.
- Durable state-entry timestamps.
- Complete activity history.
- Idempotent event handling.
- Consistent notifications.
- Shared state across all Studio screens.

Do not maintain independent copies of job workflow status in FrontDesk, Workspaces, Today, and Production.

Those screens should be different views of the same underlying records.

### Concurrency

Prevent conflicting updates when multiple employees work on the same job.

Use optimistic concurrency or the existing equivalent.

A stale assignment or workflow update must not silently overwrite a newer decision.

# 20. Final implementation objective

The completed Studio workflow must allow the business to follow a job from its initial customer booking through photographer assignment and Production without losing operational visibility.

At any point, a manager should be able to answer five questions:

**1. Where is this job?**  
The current workflow stage.

**2. Who is responsible?**  
The assigned employee, job owner, manager, or team.

**3. How long has it been there?**  
The elapsed time in the current state.

**4. Is something wrong?**  
A green, amber, or red activity indicator.

**5. What happens next?**  
The next required action, accessible through the appropriate Studio page.

The system must make those answers immediately available without requiring the manager to navigate through multiple disconnected screens.

**Final instruction to the development agent:**

Implement the complete workflow, preserving the existing Studio design and functionality. Prioritize the photographer assignment interface, multi-photographer support, connected navigation, and consistent job workflow state. Integrate all operational pages around the existing Job model. # 21. Binding decisions and implementation contracts

This section integrates the additional approved decisions. Apply it throughout Sections 1–20, automated tests, migrations, legacy status adapters, and repository documentation. Where older code or documentation describes a different process, replace that process without losing historical records. Do not ask again about routine implementation choices resolved here.

## 21.1. Permanent Unassigned Workspace and booking migration

- Seed exactly one permanent system Workspace named Unassigned under an immutable system key. Use a unique constraint and repeat-safe initialization. It must have a real Workspace ID, not a null ID, placeholder GUID, or transient queue-only record.
- FrontDesk owns access to its Pending Jobs. Ordinary workspace members must not gain access to this cross-business intake queue merely by belonging to another workspace. Administrators retain existing access.
- Existing enabled workspace email mappings route new bookings. An unknown, disabled, or ambiguous mapping routes to Unassigned and records the routing reason. Normalize addresses consistently with the existing router. Do not create workspaces from customer input.
- A website submission atomically creates or returns its Pending Job and linked intake provenance. Scope the external booking identifier by source/provider and enforce uniqueness. The same identifier and booking facts return the same Job; conflicting reuse returns a clear error. A separate booking/request record may remain for existing integrations, but it always links to this Job.
- A confirmed Job cannot remain in Unassigned. FrontDesk must route it to an enabled business workspace before confirmation. Unknown service or deposit requirements must not prevent capture of the Pending Job.
- Migrate old pending booking/request records without a Job to one Pending Job. Preserve already linked Jobs and their IDs, conversations, clients, media, schedules, and deposit references. Payment callbacks and legacy routes must resolve that same Job and must no longer create another one.
- Rerunning a migration must produce no additional Jobs, Workspaces, deposit events, conversations, crew rows, or notifications. Record provenance and reconcile malformed/ambiguous records in a migration report instead of silently discarding them.

## 21.2. Canonical transition table

One stored Job workflow stage is authoritative. Readiness substates, payment, task, crew, and shoot execution states remain separate. Use stable internal stage identifiers and map existing display strings once.

| Current stage | Entry / meaning | Responsible role | Action and next stage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pending | Booking captured; review and workspace routing required | FrontDesk; assigned claimant coordinates | Confirm eligible booking into Needs Assignment, or cancel with reason |
| Needs Assignment | Confirmation event recorded; no Job Owner | Workspace Manager | Assign Job Owner → Needs Photographer Assignment |
| Needs Photographer Assignment | Owner exists; at least one required shoot/session lacks a finalized crew | Job Owner; Workspace Manager oversees | Finalize all required crews → Awaiting Scheduling |
| Awaiting Scheduling | Required crews finalized; at least one session lacks a valid schedule/location | Job Owner or Workspace Manager | Schedule all required sessions → Scheduled |
| Scheduled | Required sessions scheduled; no required session has started | Job Owner and session leads | Start first required session → Photography In Progress |
| Photography In Progress | Required photography has started; sessions remain unfinished | Session leads; Job Owner coordinates | Finish all required sessions → Awaiting Upload |
| Awaiting Upload | Required photography finished; submissions being prepared | Assigned photographers; Job Owner coordinates | Explicitly submit ready media → Awaiting Production Review |
| Awaiting Production Review | An immutable review submission awaits inspection | Production Lead / authorized reviewer | Accept source material → Active Production; request source corrections → Changes Requested |
| Active Production | Accepted source material; assigned production tasks and quality checks underway | Production Lead and task assignees | Submit finished work → Awaiting Approval |
| Awaiting Approval | Finished output and required tasks ready for final internal approval | Existing authorized Production approver | Approve output → Ready for Delivery; request production corrections → Changes Requested |
| Changes Requested | Specific correction request with source or production target | Job Owner for source corrections; Production Lead for production corrections | Source resubmission → Awaiting Production Review; production rework → Active Production |
| Ready for Delivery | Approved output ready for existing gallery publishing / customer delivery | FrontDesk and Job Owner | Record actual delivery → Delivered |
| Delivered | Delivery recorded; final operational closure pending | Job Owner or Workspace Manager | Close after required work is resolved → Completed |
| Completed | Operational work closed | Workspace Manager | Authorized reopening only, with reason and selected applicable stage |
| Cancelled | Job cancelled with reason | Cancelling authorized role | Authorized reopening only, with reason and selected applicable stage |

Booking Confirmed is an audited event immediately entering Needs Assignment; it is not a stored stage. Needs Assignment means Needs Job Owner and can use that more descriptive text in queues. Photographer and scheduling readiness are distinct following stages.

If owner, crew, and schedule are saved together, validate each gate and record each genuine intermediate transition in the same transaction; intermediate states may have zero duration. One edit must not bypass a missing prerequisite. A no-op update never resets a clock.

When different sessions are at different points, the Job reflects the aggregate process: Photography In Progress continues until every required active session is finished. Show each session's progress alongside the Job stage. Media can be uploaded earlier without moving the Job into Production automatically.

Publishing is an audited delivery action with its existing receipt; Published is not a substitute for Delivered or Completed. Source approval is not final finished-work approval.

Migrate unambiguous legacy statuses (for example Needs Scheduling, Awaiting Upload, Needs Review, In Production, Ready for Client) to the corresponding new stage using recorded facts. Legacy Confirmed becomes a confirmation event followed by the first applicable assignment gate. Where evidence contradicts a status or approval meaning is unclear, preserve the old value and flag for reconciliation; do not fabricate completed approvals or delivered work. Preserve legacy links and adapt route/API clients together.

## 21.3. Shoot sessions and crew

- A Job contains one or more required shoot sessions. Draft sessions can exist before a date is chosen. Provide an initial draft Photography session for confirmed photography Jobs that have none.
- Each finalized session has exactly one Lead and zero or more Additional photographers. Multiple crew members are first-class assignments, not free-text notes or generic resource rows masquerading as employees.
- A photographer appears only once in the active crew of a session. The same photographer may serve in several sessions or Jobs when times permit. Leads may differ between sessions; the Job Owner remains one coordinating person.
- Store each person's role, assigned-by identity, assignment time, notes, active/cancelled status, and optional attendance window. Default attendance to the session range. Individual ranges must fall inside it.
- Scheduling requires a finalized crew, valid start/end, and a location (including a named studio or an explicit remote location). Check every active crew member and any separately reserved equipment/resources.
- Treat back-to-back attendance windows as non-overlapping. Cancelled reservations do not create conflicts. Availability and reservation conflict checks must serialize competing saves so simultaneous managers cannot double-book silently.
- Resolve conflicts by changing staff/time or, where the existing permission model allows it, an explicit manager override with the conflict list and a reason. Add a specific conflict-override permission if necessary; the default is denied. A boolean supplied by the client is never sufficient authorization. An override retains both reservations and never overwrites another Job's assignment.
- Migrate primary photographer to Lead and assistant to Additional on the same session. Preserve historical attendance, image counts, backup, reshoot, handoff, and WorkDay references. Resolve any other explicitly linked staff without duplicating them; ambiguous generic resources require reconciliation.
- Update Studio, calendars, Today, and WorkDay to read the same session/crew model. Preserve compatible old clients through an adapter where needed. Existing WorkDay acceptance must not block Studio schedules.

## 21.4. Responsibility and permission rules

| Role / relationship | Permitted operational actions |
|---|---|
| FrontDesk | Review and claim Pending Jobs; contact customers; route Pending Jobs; record deposits under existing billing permissions; confirm eligible bookings; cancel Pending Jobs with reason |
| Workspace Manager | Assign/reassign Job Owner, crew and tasks within managed workspace; schedule; resolve assignment requests; transfer eligible Jobs; oversee exceptions, cancellation and closure |
| Job Owner | Coordinate own Jobs; assign/reassign crew and tasks; schedule; review photographer readiness; submit source media for Production Review; respond to source corrections; close delivered Jobs |
| Assigned photographer | View relevant Job instructions, sessions, tasks and messages; upload media; record own session execution/readiness through existing capabilities; request assignment |
| Production Lead / existing authorized reviewer | Review source submissions; request corrections; create/assign production tasks; submit finished output; perform final approval only where existing permissions authorize it |
| Operations Manager | Cross-workspace operational oversight and explicit deposit overrides; conflict overrides only with the corresponding permission |
| Administrator | Existing administration and operational access; financial deposit overrides require the explicit Operations Manager/override capability rather than inference from an account name |

Enforce these rules at server endpoints and in the UI. Reuse existing role/permission infrastructure rather than introducing a parallel authorization system. Assigning Job Owner grants the operational capabilities necessary for that Job; it does not grant billing, refunds, role administration, or deposit override.

Assignments must grant enough Job access for recipients to see and act on their work. Reassignment/removal removes only access derived from that assignment; retain independently authorized membership and role access. Removed employees must not retain assignment-derived access indefinitely.

The Workspace Manager handles requests to become Job Owner. Other Job/task assignment requests route to the current Job Owner, with the Workspace Manager as fallback and oversight. Duplicate pending requests for the same employee/target are suppressed. Approval applies the assignment atomically and records the decision; rejection requires a brief reason; redirection selects an authorized responsible manager. Revalidate recipients when ownership changes.

A FrontDesk claim coordinates processing, not exclusive permission or permanent ownership. Allow authorized reassignment/release of a claim, audit it, and use concurrency checks.

Mock-auth Studio administrator accounts must expose and execute ordinary Job Owner, crew, task, and scheduling actions. They must not accidentally acquire deposit override merely from the login name. Preserve existing mock-auth security boundaries.

## 21.5. Replace the operational approval flow

1. Photography submissions become an explicit Source Review submission.
2. Production accepts source material or requests named corrections. Acceptance enters Active Production and creates/reuses the existing production work without duplicating tasks.
3. Production creates and assigns required tasks; those tasks appear in Today.
4. Finished work can enter Awaiting Approval only after required production tasks are Completed or explicitly Cancelled/waived by an authorized lead and current output versions satisfy existing quality checks. Unrelated administrative tasks do not automatically block production submission.
5. An existing authorized approver approves the exact submitted finished-output versions or requests corrections. Do not invent a requirement for a different approver unless current policy already requires it.
6. Final approval enters Ready for Delivery. Publishing uses approved versions and the existing provider-neutral adapter; customer delivery records method, timestamp, actor, and a reference/receipt where available. A successful publication alone does not falsely attest to other physical deliverables.
7. Required deliverables must be recorded as delivered before Delivered. Completed requires required operational tasks to be resolved. Do not introduce deferred deposit-clearance or credit gates at these stages.

Retain existing immutable media/version history, quality control, publication receipts, delivery integrations, and authorization where compatible. Replace the old state-changing routes or adapt them to these exact semantics. Update the UI labels, automation triggers, tests, and documentation together.

## 21.6. Media completion, corrections, and resubmission

- Each required session tracks required photographer submissions. Default every active assigned photographer to required; an authorized Job Owner or Workspace Manager can waive a contribution with a reason (for example, all material uploaded by the lead).
- Upload completion is recorded by batch. A photographer declares their session contribution Ready only after intended files have finished ingesting and required existing backup checks pass. Successful individual uploads never automatically dispatch Production.
- The Job Owner or Workspace Manager explicitly submits when all required sessions are finished and contributions are Ready or waived. Validate nonempty accessible media, completed ingest, and no failed/pending required files. Block partial submissions with a list of missing contributions. Do not introduce partial Job production in this phase.
- Each review submission records an immutable manifest of asset/version IDs, included sessions/contributions, submitting actor, timestamp, and revision. A new upload cannot silently alter a submitted or approved manifest.
- Production correction requests identify affected files/sessions, instructions, responsible role, and whether the correction is Source or Production. Source corrections return through a new Source Review revision; production corrections return to Active Production and then a new final Approval revision.
- Adding/replacing material after submission requires an explicit withdrawal or correction/resubmission action by the responsible authorized role. Keep prior revisions and approvals as historical records; do not treat new versions as approved.
- Deduplicate by Job, submission revision and event type. One readiness event creates one review queue entry and notification set; retries of that event create neither extra tasks nor notifications. A genuine new revision produces a new, linked notification.

## 21.7. Regular-clock timing for this phase

Use UTC for storage and elapsed calculations; use the configured Studio display timezone, default America/Jamaica. Display hours/days continuously across nights, weekends, and holidays. Business-hours calendars and pause-adjusted service clocks are explicitly deferred.

Persist stage-entry time and stage-transition history atomically with each real stage change. Time in stage is always now minus stage-entry time for an active stage. Historical durations use the recorded exit time. Do not reset timestamps for viewing, notes, assignments that do not change stage, notifications, restarts, or repeated commands.

Proposed editable defaults, not agreed business service levels:

| Stage | Amber after | Red after |
|---|---|---|
| Pending (including Unassigned) | 2 hours | 8 hours |
| Needs Assignment | 4 hours | 24 hours |
| Needs Photographer Assignment | 4 hours | 24 hours |
| Awaiting Scheduling | 8 hours | 48 hours |
| Awaiting Upload | 8 hours | 24 hours |
| Awaiting Production Review | 4 hours | 24 hours |
| Active Production | 24 hours | 72 hours |
| Awaiting Approval | 24 hours | 72 hours |
| Changes Requested | 8 hours | 24 hours |
| Ready for Delivery | 24 hours | 72 hours |
| Delivered (awaiting closure) | 24 hours | 72 hours |

Scheduled and Photography In Progress use session deadlines instead of stage age: for the earliest required unfinished session, amber begins one hour after planned end and red begins four hours after planned end. Before that deadline, a future scheduled session remains Normal irrespective of how long the Job has been Scheduled. Recompute the next relevant deadline as sessions finish; rescheduling changes the deadline and is audited but does not reset time in the unchanged Job stage.

Boundary behavior: Green below warning; Amber at or above warning and below overdue; Red at or above overdue. Validate positive warning and overdue greater than warning. Administrators may change thresholds; changes affect current evaluations and are audited without rewriting history. Overdue First sorts overdue items first, then by time beyond the applicable deadline; unknown timing remains clearly identifiable.

Completed and Cancelled use Grey / Inactive and freeze their stage duration at closure. A separate Blocked flag with reason, responsible person, and review date highlights impediments without silently pausing the ordinary clock.

Legacy stage entry can be backfilled only from reliable transition evidence. Otherwise leave it unknown, show Timing unavailable with a neutral indicator, and record a separate Monitoring started timestamp. Do not imply Normal, Overdue, or a fabricated historical duration. The next real transition creates a trustworthy timestamp. Any unsupported timing policy must be rejected rather than silently evaluated as business hours.

## 21.8. Simplified deposit rule and explicit deferrals

For this phase, any authorized recorded positive deposit makes a Pending Job eligible for FrontDesk confirmation, regardless of the configured required deposit. Preserve existing financial amounts, receipts, currency validation, links, and repeat-safe posting. Recording money enables confirmation; it does not automatically confirm or create a new Job.

Without a recorded positive deposit, the existing brief's Operations Manager override path permits confirmation. Record manager identity, timestamp, reason and original requirement, and preserve unpaid balances. Permit zero or unknown required-deposit metadata at intake; do not create a new zero-deposit automatic-confirmation path. Such bookings can proceed through a recorded deposit or an explicit override for now.

Do not implement minimum deposit calculation, provider verification, partial-payment eligibility rules, credit privileges, customer account terms, refund policy, automated chargebacks, or retroactive override revocation. Keep existing financial behavior available; cancellation directs any financial follow-up to existing FrontDesk/Billing processes. Keep this simplified rule explicit and replaceable later. Replayed receipts do not create duplicate financial entries; a receipt reused with conflicting facts returns an error.

## 21.9. Exception and transfer rules

- Rescheduling: authorized owner/manager changes session dates and crew with conflict checks, an audit entry, and employee notifications. Changing a future date does not erase the existing stage's duration. Started/completed session times retain actual history; correcting actual attendance uses existing authorized correction processes.
- No-show: record a session exception and notify the Job Owner/manager; do not automatically complete, cancel, or refund the Job. Management chooses rescheduling or cancellation.
- Reshoot: add a required linked session with a reason. Before Production submission, aggregate readiness reflects the new required session. After submission, explicitly withdraw/reopen the affected review under authorized correction controls before changing downstream readiness.
- Removing a lead or cancelling a required session before execution forces a crew/schedule readiness recheck. Do not allow a finalized session with no lead. Replace a required session or waive that requirement with a reason; cancellation alone is not proof the photography obligation was fulfilled.
- Job cancellation: FrontDesk may cancel Pending Jobs; Workspace Manager or Operations Manager may cancel confirmed active Jobs. Audit the reason, release unstarted reservations, cancel/suspend relevant open tasks, remove the Job from active queues, notify affected employees, and retain all historical media, payments and completed work. Do not erase or silently unpublish an existing customer gallery; flag its existing unpublish action for the authorized operator.
- Blocked work: record a reason and responsible party; show the flag in Jobs and Today. The regular clock keeps running. Avoid a separate timer-pausing On Hold stage in this phase.
- Workspace transfer: FrontDesk routes Pending Jobs; confirmed Jobs require Operations Manager authority or both source and destination managers' recorded approval. Lock/version-check the Job, record transfer, and notify both sides. The destination manager becomes accountable. Retain explicit Job Owner and crew assignments and Job-specific grants unless deliberately reassigned; inherited workspace access follows the destination. Recheck readiness, request routing and permissions. Transfer changes the stage only if prerequisites genuinely become unmet.
- Reopening: only Workspace Manager or Operations Manager may reopen a closed Job, with a reason and an applicable valid stage. Start a new stage occurrence without deleting prior closure, delivery, payment, approval, or publication history.

## 21.10. Navigation, concurrency and notification contracts

Navigation:
- Reuse the existing Pipeline destination for Jobs/Workflow Monitoring or provide a compatible route alias. Do not add a second competing board with separate Job state.
- Publish a logical destination → concrete route/tab/action mapping. Each assignment, schedule, review, task, gallery and delivery screen must retain Job/Workspace context and a reliable route to Today.
- Link crew editing from Job → Assignments → Assign Photographers. Today and Workspace actions deep-link there. Keep existing Subjects/collections, gallery, clients, messages, billing and WorkDay screens accessible.
- Preserve list filters, selected workspace, sort, pagination and scroll on return. Refresh changed rows without losing list context. Unauthorized deep links show a clear access message and working navigation.

Concurrency:
- Use a Job revision/version token for state, owner, crew, workspace and relevant readiness changes; use session/task revision tokens where independently edited. Expected status alone does not detect two conflicting edits in the same stage.
- Apply validation, changes, audit and durable event enqueueing in one transaction. Reject stale writes with a clear conflict response and refresh option, preserving newer data. Never silently retry an obsolete assignment decision.
- Serialize resource reservations across all scheduling writers and validate permissions at commit time. Idempotency keys match target, action and submitted facts; conflicting reuse is rejected.

Notifications:
- Extend the existing durable notification/event infrastructure; do not create a parallel service. Commit an outbox/event entry with the business change, then deliver asynchronously through existing infrastructure.
- Resolve actual employee identities from existing roles, assignments, and team configuration. FrontDesk, Production and responsible-manager audiences must be configured; missing recipients appear as an actionable delivery/configuration failure, never as silent success.
- Use a stable event ID plus recipient/channel for deduplication. Preserve delivery attempts, failures and retry status. A delivery failure does not roll back completed business work or cause duplicate Jobs/tasks when retried.
- Trigger overdue notifications once per stage occurrence, threshold crossing and recipient; repeat reminders are configurable and off by default. A new stage occurrence can alert again. Unknown historical timing does not generate invented overdue alerts.
- Include direct Job/task links; respect current permissions when opening them. Capture intended recipients and revalidate authorization before delivery, suppressing superseded assignments where appropriate.
- Guarantee durable enqueueing and deduplication under Studio's control; do not claim external email exactly-once delivery if the existing provider cannot guarantee it. Test crash/retry boundaries and retain provider correlation IDs where supported.
